Etsy SEO in 2026: titles, tags and attributes explained

A plain-English guide to getting your listings found on Etsy, with no tricks or hacks, just how search actually works and what to do about it.

Ayomide Joshua11 November 2026, 9 min read

How Etsy search works, in one minute

When a shopper searches on Etsy, two things happen:

  1. Matching. Etsy finds listings that are relevant to the search, using your title, tags, category and attributes.
  2. Ranking. Etsy orders those listings using signals such as how shoppers interact with them (clicks, favourites, purchases), how complete and well-reviewed your shop is, shipping, and how well the listing fits that particular shopper.

Etsy SEO is about doing well at both: being matched to the right searches, then being the listing shoppers choose.

Step 1: find the words buyers actually use

Good keywords come from buyers, not from you. Start by typing your product into the Etsy search bar and noting the suggestions that appear: those are real searches. Look at the top listings for those searches and the words they use. Then check your own Etsy Stats to see which searches already bring visitors to your shop.

Aim for a mix of:

  • Core phrases: what the product is ("ceramic mug", "wedding planner printable").
  • Descriptive phrases: style, material, colour ("speckled stoneware mug").
  • Intent phrases: who it's for and why ("gift for coffee lover", "bridesmaid proposal").

Specific phrases usually have less competition and more motivated buyers than broad ones.

Step 2: write a clear, buyer-first title

Titles can be up to 140 characters, but longer isn't better. Etsy's own guidance is to keep titles short and readable, under about 15 words, and to put extra details such as recipient and occasion in your tags instead. Shoppers skim titles on small screens.

A simple formula that works: what it is + key detail + who or what it's for.

Before: Mug, Coffee Mug, Ceramic Mug, Handmade Mug, Pottery Mug, Gift Mug, Mug For Him

After: Handmade Speckled Ceramic Coffee Mug, Stoneware Pottery Gift for Coffee Lovers

Step 3: use all 13 tags well

Tags are where you capture the searches your title doesn't cover. A few rules:

  • Use all 13. Each empty tag is a missed chance to be found.
  • Use phrases, not single words. Tags can be up to 20 characters.
  • Don't waste tags on words already covered by your category or attributes.
  • Cover different angles: product, style, recipient, occasion and material.
  • Think about regional spelling where relevant ("personalised" and "personalized").

Step 4: fill in every attribute

Attributes (colour, material, occasion, holiday, style and more) are used when shoppers filter their results. They also help Etsy match your listing to searches. Choose the most specific category you can, then complete every attribute that applies.

Step 5: write descriptions for humans

Your description is where browsers become buyers. Open with a sentence or two that describes the item in natural language, including your main keywords. Then answer the questions buyers ask most: size, materials, personalisation options, processing time and care. Your listings can also appear in Google search, where the description helps.

Step 6: earn the click and the sale

Because ranking depends on how shoppers respond, the parts of your listing that convince people matter for SEO too:

  • A bright, clear first photo that stands out at thumbnail size
  • A full set of photos, including scale, detail and the product in use
  • Competitive pricing and shipping
  • Good reviews, and replies to messages that come quickly

Common Etsy SEO mistakes

  • Stuffing titles with repeated keywords
  • Copying a competitor's tags without checking they fit your item
  • Using only very broad keywords such as "necklace" or "art"
  • Changing every listing at once, so you can't see what worked
  • Ignoring Etsy Stats, the best free keyword tool you have

How long does Etsy SEO take?

Changes don't show overnight. Give updated listings a few weeks before judging them, and compare views and visits for the same listing before and after. The shops that grow are the ones that improve steadily, not the ones that chase quick tricks.
