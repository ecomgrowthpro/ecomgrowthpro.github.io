Shopify store getting visitors but no sales? 7 things to check

Traffic without sales is one of the most frustrating problems in e-commerce. The good news: it usually comes down to a handful of fixable issues.

Ayomide Joshua11 November 2026, 8 min read

When a store gets visitors but no orders, owners often respond by spending more on ads. That usually makes the problem more expensive, not smaller. If people arrive and leave without buying, more traffic just means more people leaving.

Before you spend another dollar on ads, work through these seven checks. They're the same ones I start with in every Shopify audit.

1. Are the right people visiting?

Not all traffic is equal. Visitors from a broad ad, a giveaway, or a viral video that isn't really about your product may never have intended to buy.

How to check: in Shopify, go to Analytics and look at sessions and conversion rate by traffic source. If one source brings lots of visits and almost no add-to-carts, the problem is who you're attracting, not your store.

2. Does your store load fast on a phone?

Most e-commerce traffic is mobile. A slow store loses people before they've seen a single product. Too many apps, huge images and heavy themes are the usual causes.

How to fix: remove apps you don't use (and check they didn't leave code behind), compress images, and test your homepage and a product page with Google PageSpeed Insights.

3. Does your product page answer every question?

Shoppers can't touch your product or ask a salesperson. Your product page has to do that job. Common gaps: too few photos, no sizing or dimensions, vague descriptions and no clear delivery time.

How to fix: show the product from several angles and in use. Write descriptions that start with the benefit, then the details. Add sizing, materials, care instructions and expected delivery dates near the Add to cart button.

4. Do you look trustworthy?

A new visitor is asking one question: "Is this store real, and will I get my order?" A template that looks unfinished, no reviews, no contact details or missing policies all raise doubt.

How to fix: add product reviews, a real About page, clear shipping and returns policies, contact details and secure payment badges. Make sure there's no placeholder text left over from your theme.

5. Are shipping costs a surprise at checkout?

Research on cart abandonment, such as Baymard Institute's long-running studies, consistently finds unexpected extra costs at checkout among the top reasons people leave without buying.

How to fix: show shipping costs early, on the product page or in an announcement bar. A free-shipping threshold ("Free shipping over $50") can also nudge order values up.

6. Is checkout easy?

Every extra step loses some buyers. Forcing account creation, limited payment options or a confusing cart page all cost sales.

How to fix: allow guest checkout, turn on accelerated options such as Shop Pay, Apple Pay and Google Pay where available, and offer the payment methods your customers actually use in their country.

7. Are you following up with people who almost bought?

Many visitors add to cart and leave, then forget. Without follow-up, those sales are simply lost.

How to fix: turn on Shopify's abandoned checkout emails, or set up a short abandoned cart flow in your email app. A friendly reminder with the product photo often recovers sales you'd otherwise never see.

Where to start

If you only have an hour, start with 3 and 5. Clear product pages and no surprise shipping costs fix more stores than anything else. Then check your traffic sources, so you know whether the issue is your store or your audience.
