Why your Etsy listings aren't showing up in search (and how to fix it)

You've made something great, listed it, and… nothing. No views, no favourites, no sales. Here's why that happens, and what to change.

Ayomide Joshua11 November 2026, 7 min read

Most Etsy sellers who message me about low views assume Etsy is "hiding" their shop, or that they need to pay for ads. Usually, neither is true. In almost every shop I review, the products are good, but the listings aren't giving Etsy what it needs to match them with buyers.

Etsy search works in two stages. First, it finds every listing that is relevant to what the buyer typed. Then it ranks those listings, based on things like how often shoppers click, favourite and buy them, and how good your shop's customer experience is. If you fail the first stage, you never get the chance to compete in the second.

Here are the eight problems I see most often.

1. Your title describes the product the way you see it, not the way buyers search

A seller might title a necklace "Luna Dream Pendant". It's a lovely name, but nobody searches for it. Buyers type things like "gold moon necklace" or "dainty crescent necklace gift for her".

The fix: lead your title with the plain words a buyer would type, then add one or two important details such as material, style or occasion. Etsy recommends short, readable titles (under about 15 words), so skip long strings of repeated keywords. Keep your product's "brand name" for the description.

2. You're not using all 13 tags, or your tags repeat your title

Each listing gets 13 tags, and each one is another chance to match a search. I regularly see shops using five or six tags, or tags that just repeat the exact words already in the title.

The fix: use all 13. Use multi-word phrases (tags can be up to 20 characters), and cover different ways people search: the product, the style, the recipient ("gift for sister"), the occasion ("bridesmaid gift") and the material.

3. Attributes and category are left empty

Attributes like colour, material, occasion and style are used to filter search results. If a buyer filters for "silver" and your listing has no colour attribute, you're invisible to them, even if your title says silver.

The fix: pick the most specific category available, and fill in every attribute that applies. Attributes also count as matches, so you don't need to waste tags repeating them.

4. Your thumbnail doesn't win the click

Even when your listing does appear in search, it has to be clicked. Etsy watches how shoppers respond to listings, so a thumbnail people scroll past can hold your ranking back.

The fix: make the first photo bright, sharp and instantly clear at a small size. Show the product, not the background. Then check how it looks in the search results on a phone, next to your competitors.

5. Your shipping looks expensive

Shipping cost is one of the first things buyers compare. A listing that looks cheaper but adds high shipping often loses to one with a slightly higher price and free or low-cost shipping. Etsy also gives some extra visibility to US listings that offer free shipping on orders over $35.

The fix: consider building some of the shipping cost into your price, and check your processing times are realistic and competitive.

6. Your shop looks unfinished

Etsy factors in the customer and shop experience. A missing About section, empty policies, no shop announcement or no profile photo all make a shop look less trustworthy, both to Etsy and to buyers.

The fix: complete every section of your shop: banner, icon, About story, policies and FAQs. It takes an afternoon and helps every listing.

7. You're targeting searches that are far too competitive

If your only keywords are "necklace" or "t-shirt", you're competing with hundreds of thousands of listings. New shops rarely win those searches.

The fix: go more specific. "Personalised name necklace for mom" has far less competition, and the people searching it are much closer to buying.

8. You're expecting results overnight

After you change a listing, Etsy needs time to process it and see how buyers respond. Changing titles every few days makes it hard to tell what's working.

The fix: make thoughtful changes, then give them a few weeks. Track views and visits for each listing in your Etsy Stats so you can compare before and after.

Quick checklist

  • Title starts with what buyers actually search
  • All 13 tags used, as multi-word phrases
  • Most specific category, every attribute filled in
  • A thumbnail that's clear on a phone screen
  • Competitive shipping and processing times
  • A complete shop: About, policies, banner and icon
